# Treatment: "Rain Day" — watercolor, 30 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps, English

## Candidate structures
1. **One long descent**: the rain's path from cloud to stream, one continuous tilt down the mountain (a transect). Clean, but it is the style's default grammar and the village is only scenery.
2. **A journal page, one day in three entries**: morning / noon / evening, each painted over the last. Too episodic for 30 s.
3. **One drop, one umbrella, one lit window** *(chosen)*: a single blue drop of wet pigment blooms into the storm; the camera tilts down the village while the rain recolours it (palette as data); one vermilion umbrella walks home along the stone path; the rain holds its breath for one beat; the walker reaches a door and the only unpainted paper, a window, becomes the light. Chosen because it gives one subject, one want (get home), one turn (the pause) and one echo (the first drop / the last drop), and the accent colour has a job.

## Logline and arc
A village in the mountains takes the rain; one red umbrella walks home through it, and the pause between two downpours is where the film turns.
- Setup (0–11 s): blank sheet, one drop, cloud, ridges, rain.
- Turn (11–22 s): the village paints in and darkens; umbrella walks; at 18.5 s the rain stops, music out, a single eave drop.
- Ending (22–30 s): the door opens, the window stays paper-white and warm, push into the window, everything else mists back to paper; last drop falls onto the blank sheet.

## Benchmark
Learn: field-journal plates (specimen + hand note), handscroll panoramas (one continuous world, mist as a divider), Hiroshige's rain-lined *Sudden Shower* grammar (diagonal strokes as the sky itself). Do not take: any specific composition, figure, or print layout.
